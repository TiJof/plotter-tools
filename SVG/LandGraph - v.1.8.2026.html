What LandGraph is

LandGraph generates landscapes made only of single-stroke lines, so every mark can be drawn by a pen plotter (AxiDraw, iDraw, NextDraw or any plotter that accepts SVG). There are no fills: tone comes from hatching, contour lines and stippled dots.

Everything that sits behind something else is hidden automatically, so a tree in front of a mountain really covers it on paper. Each pen colour is exported as its own layer, ready to plot one pen after another.

Quick start

  1. In the left panel, under Style, choose a Main style (for example Scenic Landscapes) and a Substyle (for example Mountain lake).
  2. Under Paper, pick the sheet size and orientation you will plot on.
  3. Press Local Random until you like the composition. Fine-tune with the sliders in the left panel.
  4. Press ★ Save to favourites to keep it.
  5. Press Save SVG (all pens in one file, one layer per pen) or One SVG per pen (.zip), then plot.

Local Random vs General Random

Local Random keeps your style, substyle and all your terrain and element settings. It changes:

  • the composition — the shape of the terrain, where trees, houses and rocks fall, the shape of every cloud;
  • the sky — sun, moon or nothing, the sun or moon style, its position and size, the number and type of clouds, occasional sky lines, and stars at night.

Use it when you like the recipe and want another variation of it.

General Random randomises every option of the current style (terrain, hatching, elements, water, sky), plus paper size, orientation, margin, the number of pens, their colours and what each pen draws. It never mixes styles: a General Random in Oceania stays Oceania. If it picks a single pen, that pen is always black; with two or more pens the colours can be anything.

After either button the left panel shows the new values, and the substyle switches to — custom —.

Styles & substyles

The Main style decides the kind of world; each has its own terrain, its own allowed elements and its own option panels. The Substyle is a ready-made starting point inside that style. Each style remembers its own settings when you switch to another one and back. Reset style (in Plotter & settings) returns the current style to its first substyle.

Option panels

Panels open and close with a click on their title. Only options that make sense for the current style are shown.

  • Paper — sheet format (A5 to A2, Letter, 30×40 cm), orientation, margin, an optional frame and the signature.
  • Land / Mountains / Terrain — the landform (where a style offers several), the number of depth planes (more planes = more depth), relief height, sharpness, number of peaks or hills, ridge detail, snow and the white gap kept between planes so lines never touch.
  • Hatching — contour lines, slope hatching or both; spacing in mm; how many contour lines; stroke length; and the tone of far and near planes (far planes lighter gives atmospheric depth).
  • Elements (named after the style, e.g. Village, Jungle, Skyscrapers) — counts, densities, sizes and types of everything the style can contain.
  • Trees & vegetation — the overall Tree & vegetation density (0–200%, scales trees, grass, reeds and cattails together), the allowed tree types, forest density and size.
  • Water — type, horizon line, reflection, waves, line spacing and density, and the foreground shore.
  • Sky — sun, moon or none; sun style (outline, stripes, rings, rays); moon style (crescent, crescent with earthshine, half, gibbous, full moon); position, height and radius; clouds and their style; sky lines; stars; birds (drawn as simple outlines).
  • Plotter & settings — drawing and travel speed (used only for the time estimate), Export / Import settings and Reset style.

Pens & colours

Under Pens choose 1 to 8 pens. For each pen set the colour, a name (used for layer names and file names) and the line width in mm (used for the preview and the SVG stroke width).

Under What each pen draws, assign every part of the drawing — mountain outlines, far and near hatching, trees, rocks, water, sun or moon, sky, buildings, windows and details, birds, frame, signature — to a pen. The list adapts to the current style.

Saving & exporting

  • SVG files use real millimetres, so they open at the correct size in Inkscape or your plotter software. Each pen is an Inkscape layer named “number + pen name”.
  • Export settings saves the current style's settings as a small file; Import settings brings it back exactly (same image, same code).
  • File names include the signature code, e.g. LandGraph-ABC-123.svg.

Favourites

  • ★ Save to favourites keeps the current image; saving the same image twice does not create a duplicate.
  • Open favourites shows the catalog in the work area. Click a thumbnail or Open to load that image back into the editor and keep adjusting it; ← Back to drawing returns without changes.
  • Rename a favourite by typing in its name field. ✕ deletes it (press twice to confirm).
  • Export (one) or Export all creates a file you can keep as a backup or send to another LandGraph user, who adds it with Import favourites.
  • Favourites are kept on this device. Export them to keep a backup or to move them to another computer.

Plotting tips

  • Plot the layers in the order listed in SVG individual, changing pens between passes. Lighter colours first often looks best.
  • The time estimate uses the speeds in Plotter & settings; set them to your plotter's values for a closer estimate.
  • Ballpoints and fineliners give the cleanest result. With paint markers, increase hatching spacing so lines don't merge.
  • If a drawing is too dense for your pen, raise Spacing in Hatching, lower contour lines, or reduce element densities.

Natural rules

LandGraph keeps scenes believable on its own, whatever the sliders say:

  • Each style has its own terrain: no mountains in cities, deserts or Oceania; dunes only in deserts; ice only at the poles.
  • Houses, churches, yurts, igloos and tents are placed only on low, fairly flat ground — never on peaks or steep slopes.
  • Trees follow altitude: only pines and shrubs grow high up, birches and broadleaf trees lower, poplars only on low flat ground; nothing grows on snow.
  • Cattails grow only on lake shores; palms only on beaches, islands, oases and in the tropics; cacti only in the American desert.
  • Compact elements (a tree, a house, a building, a rock, a boat) are always shown whole — anything that would be cut by the edge of the image is left out.
  • The sun, the moon and clouds are always kept fully inside the image.

Signature

Every image carries LandGraph - ABC 12345 in the bottom-right corner: three random letters and a number of one to five digits, unique to the image. It is drawn with single strokes, so the plotter writes it too. It sits in the bottom margin when there is room, otherwise just inside the drawing in a small clear area. You can hide it under Paper; General Random always turns it back on.

Favourites

Favourites are kept on this device. Export one (or all) to a file to back them up or send them to another LandGraph user, who can bring them in with Import favourites.
